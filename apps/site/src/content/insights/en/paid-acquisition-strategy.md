Paid acquisition should buy a decision before it buys scale. The first question is not “Which platform is cheapest?” It is “Can we tell whether this channel brings the right customer to a path that produces value?”

In Iris Wei's self-reported workshops, teams often listed many channels but lacked one complete measurement chain. Each channel had activity, yet none could explain cost per qualified action or paid outcome. Adding budget would have increased ambiguity.

## Paid acquisition readiness checklist

| Gate | Ready means | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | One observable audience and job | Interview and narrow |
| Offer | Clear outcome, price, and boundary | Fix positioning |
| Landing page | Message matches the ad and intent | Build one path |
| Conversion | Meaningful event is instrumented | Configure and test |
| Economics | Revenue and delivery cost are understood | Run paid pilot first |
| Capacity | Product and team can serve demand | Limit or postpone |
| Test design | Budget, duration, hypothesis, stop rule | Write experiment brief |

## 1. Start with one segment and one job

Do not target “all startups.” Define a role, company state, recent trigger, and job. Creative, keyword, landing page, and offer should describe the same situation.

Use the [competitive analysis template](/en/insights/competitive-analysis-template-startups) to understand the customer's alternatives before bidding on category language.

## 2. Choose a conversion that represents progress

A click is not a business conversion. Depending on the model, the meaningful event may be a qualified demo request, completed report, verified installation, paid pilot, or purchase.

Google's official documentation explains that website conversion measurement records defined actions after ad interactions and requires correct tags and event configuration. Review [Google Ads conversion tracking](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7521212?hl=en) and test the implementation before interpreting campaign performance.

Record identity, consent, cross-domain behavior, attribution window, deduplication, and offline follow-up. Measurement gaps should stay visible.

## 3. Match the landing page to the campaign

The ad promise, page headline, proof, CTA, and post-click experience should belong to one intent. Sending every campaign to the homepage makes it difficult to distinguish weak traffic from a weak page.

For high-intent searches, answer the question early, show relevant proof and constraints, and provide one primary next action. Do not hide price or eligibility if they determine fit.

## 4. Run the smallest decision-quality test

Write the test before spending:

```text
Segment and trigger:
Channel and placement:
Offer and landing page:
Primary conversion:
Guardrail metrics:
Budget and duration:
Minimum useful sample:
Stop conditions:
Decision date and owner:
```

There is no universal first budget. It must be large enough to answer a defined question but small enough that failure is affordable. A tiny test with no possible conversions is not conservative; it is inconclusive.

## 5. Calculate economics by segment and channel

Keep spend, qualified conversions, payment, refunds, delivery cost, and retention connected. If sales close offline, import or reconcile that evidence rather than judging only form fills.

Do not combine unlike segments into one average. The same channel can work for one job and fail for another.

## 6. Scale only the part that worked

If one audience, creative, keyword group, or landing page produced qualified outcomes, expand that unit gradually. Preserve a holdout or comparable baseline where practical and watch whether conversion quality changes with volume.

If activation is weak, use the [customer onboarding best practices](/en/insights/customer-onboarding-best-practices). If willingness to pay is unclear, use the [monetization strategy](/en/insights/monetization-strategy). Paid reach cannot repair either problem by itself.

## Stop or pause when

- tracking is broken or definitions changed;
- the landing page does not match the campaign;
- qualified users cannot reach first value;
- delivery capacity is saturated;
- cost is rising while downstream quality falls;
- the result is too small to support a decision;
- the campaign depends on unsupported claims.

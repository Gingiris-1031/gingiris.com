# Productize Consulting Expertise: From 80 Clients to 22 Playbooks

A woman I had added on WeChat six or seven years earlier suddenly contacted me. She studies astrophysics and was trying to turn that expertise into an industry product. After reading my posts for a long time, she wanted a paid consultation.

The message made me pause. We had barely spoken when we first connected. A dormant relationship had become relevant years later because the work I published made it clear what I could now help with.

This is not an article about acquisition tactics. It is about the layer underneath: once you have delivered enough one-to-one work, how do you turn the repeated parts into something other people can use without you in the room?

## One-to-one work comes first

After leaving AFFiNE in May 2024, I spent months talking with companies. Some conversations began as job exploration and became requests for advisory help. Later, I ran both ongoing company advisory work and individual sessions.

By October 2026, I had worked with more than 80 companies in some capacity. That number is self-reported and covers different depths of work. It does not mean every company received the same engagement or result.

The useful pattern was repetition. Clients kept asking how to launch an open-source product, plan Product Hunt, find early users, evaluate competitors, negotiate creators, and package a service.

By the fifth explanation I still enjoyed the conversation. By the thirtieth, I understood that refusing to document it would keep the business trapped in one-to-one repetition.

## The three stages of productizing experience

**Stage one: internal notes.** Record the question, context, answer, action, and what happened. These notes can be messy. Their purpose is evidence, not publication.

**Stage two: client execution tools.** Turn repeated decisions into checklists, timelines, worksheets, and templates that a client can use during delivery. If a step cannot be acted on, it is still advice—not an operating manual.

**Stage three: an independent knowledge product.** Consolidate the material, add missing context, define who it is for, remove client-confidential detail, and make the package usable without a live explanation.

My own library eventually reached 22 guides. That inventory grew through repeated projects; it was not designed in one sitting.

| Layer | Primary user | Output | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal notes | Consultant | Decisions and outcomes | Can I find the evidence later? |
| Delivery tools | Current client | Checklist, template, timeline | Can the client execute it? |
| Knowledge product | Independent buyer | Structured manual and examples | Can they use it without a call? |
| Advisory service | Context-specific client | Judgment and adaptation | Does it fit this situation? |

## Three mistakes I made

The first was trying to cover everything. The result was a large collection of half-finished documents. I improved the system by completing the five questions clients asked most often before expanding.

The second was writing abstract advice. “Find the right channel and create high-converting content” sounds reasonable but cannot be executed. An operating manual needs the next observable action, the required input, and a way to tell whether the step is complete.

The third was treating pricing as a one-time theoretical decision. I started with a small material pack and raised the price as the scope, evidence, and usability changed. Those sales are my own history, not a pricing benchmark for another consultant.

The guide on [consulting pricing strategy](/en/insights/consulting-pricing-strategy) explains how I think about that decision without pretending one formula fits everyone.

## The product and the service strengthen each other

A manual can answer the repeated question. Advisory work handles the exception: “This step does not fit our buyer, team, or product—what should we do instead?”

The knowledge product creates shared language before the call. The call produces new edge cases. Those cases improve the manual after permission and evidence checks. That is the real flywheel.

Referral partnerships often fail after the introduction, not before it. One person remembers a promised percentage, another remembers a discount, and nobody defined whether the fee applies to the first invoice, collected revenue, renewals, refunds, or related accounts.

The fix is not a complicated commission system. It is a short written agreement that makes qualification, attribution, payment, and disclosure explicit.

## Referral fee agreement checklist

| Term | Decision to record |
|---|---|
| Qualified referral | What information and consent must be provided? |
| Exclusions | Existing pipeline, prior contacts, employees, restricted accounts |
| Attribution window | How long after introduction can a fee be earned? |
| Fee basis | Fixed amount, percentage, margin, or collected revenue |
| Payment event | Signature, invoice, cleared funds, or milestone |
| Adjustments | Refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, taxes, credits |
| Future revenue | Renewals, upsells, affiliates, related entities |
| Conduct | Approved claims, privacy, anti-spam, brand use |
| Disclosure | When the financial relationship must be visible |
| Records | Evidence, reporting, invoice, dispute process |
| Termination | Existing referrals, survival, final payments |

## 1. Define a qualified referral

An introduction should identify the prospect, confirm permission to connect, and provide enough context for a relevant follow-up. Decide whether a name, email, meeting, or completed discovery call qualifies.

Exclude accounts already in the pipeline or contacted within a defined lookback window. Without this rule, two people can claim the same opportunity.

## 2. Separate introduction from sales work

A simple introduction, active qualification, co-selling, content promotion, event delivery, and account management are different contributions. Price and responsibilities should reflect the actual work.

If the referrer is expected to make claims about the service, specify approved statements and evidence. Do not pay for misleading endorsements or hidden customer pressure.

## 3. Define the fee basis and payment event

Possible structures include a fixed amount, a percentage of the first collected payment, a share of defined project revenue, or a separately priced delivery role. State whether taxes, reimbursed expenses, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks are included.

Iris has self-reported using a simple 10% rule in some historical consulting referrals because it was easy to explain and left most revenue with the delivery party. That experience does not establish a market rate. Margin, involvement, regulation, and risk can produce a different answer.

## 4. Write the attribution window

Define when attribution begins, how long it lasts, and what happens if the prospect buys a different service months later. Also address renewals, upsells, subsidiaries, and multiple referrers.

The goal is not to predict every edge case. It is to prevent the most valuable cases from depending on memory.

## 5. Handle disclosure and prohibited activity

The US Federal Trade Commission explains that a material connection that could affect how an endorsement is evaluated may require clear disclosure. Review the FTC's [Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/advertisement-endorsements) when a referral relationship includes public promotion to US consumers.

Other jurisdictions, industries, professions, procurement systems, broker activities, healthcare, finance, employment, and public-sector sales may impose different or stricter rules. Obtain qualified local advice.

## 6. Protect customer trust and data

Do not transfer personal details without a lawful basis and appropriate consent. Define what the referrer may share, where records are stored, how long they are retained, and who handles deletion or access requests.

State that the referrer cannot promise pricing, outcomes, availability, or contract terms unless explicitly authorized.

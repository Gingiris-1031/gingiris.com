My historical pricing moved from roughly RMB 800 per hour to RMB 1,500 per hour, with a monthly advisory option around RMB 7,000. Those figures show one offer evolving; they do not tell another consultant what to charge.

| Situation | Better starting model | Define before quoting | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| One bounded question | Paid diagnostic | Preparation, call, memo | Scope expands after the call |
| Repeated founder decisions | Monthly advisory | Cadence, response time, topics | Unlimited access expectation |
| Fixed implementation | Project fee | Deliverables and acceptance | Hidden dependencies |
| Team alignment | Workshop package | Participants and outputs | Training without a decision |
| Unclear problem | Discovery first | Evidence needed | Quoting before diagnosis |

## Why does hourly pricing feel difficult at the beginning?

An early consultant often asks, “What is one hour of my time worth?” That question is almost impossible to answer because the client is not buying an hour in the abstract. They are buying a change in confidence, direction, or execution.

Replace it with four questions: What decision is blocked? What is the cost of making it late or badly? What information must be reviewed before advising? What concrete artifact remains after the engagement?

The same 60-minute meeting can be casual conversation or the final step in a high-value diagnosis. Pricing should reflect the work and responsibility around the meeting, not only its duration.

## When should you use a paid diagnostic?

A paid diagnostic works when the question is bounded enough to prepare for and answer without ongoing execution. A strong package includes a pre-call questionnaire, review of relevant product and growth materials, a focused session, a short written decision memo, three priorities, three explicit non-priorities, and one follow-up clarification window.

Do not call it “one hour of consulting.” Name the decision: “launch positioning diagnostic,” “open-source growth review,” or “founder channel prioritization.”

## When is a monthly advisory retainer appropriate?

A retainer fits a client who faces recurring decisions and benefits from continuity. It should not mean unlimited messaging.

Define the number and length of sessions, asynchronous response expectations, in-scope decisions, required client inputs, included artifacts, and what triggers a separate project. The value is accumulated context: the advisor remembers prior decisions, notices contradictions, and helps the founder avoid reopening the same question every week.

## How do you raise consulting prices without bluffing?

Raise the price when the offer becomes clearer and the evidence improves—not because a social post says you are undercharging.

| Signal | What it may indicate |
|---|---|
| Strong close rate from qualified leads | Price may be below willingness to pay |
| Repeated requests for the same outcome | Offer can be standardized |
| More preparation than scope assumes | Price or scope is too low |
| Clients reuse the memo | Artifact has durable value |
| Demand exceeds delivery capacity | Filtering and pricing need revision |

Change one variable at a time. Quote the new price to the next qualified segment, track objections, and review several conversations. Do not rewrite old outcomes to justify an increase.

## What should never be included by default?

Ambiguity is the most expensive line item in consulting. State exclusions plainly: implementation unless specified, emergency access, unlimited revisions, stakeholder alignment outside the named group, guaranteed traffic or revenue, and work that depends on data the client cannot provide.

Boundaries protect the client as much as the consultant. They make the price comparable to a real deliverable.

## How should a solo consultant combine revenue streams?

My founder deck described a historical mix of roughly 70% enterprise monthly advisory, 15% consultations and workshops, and 15% courses. This mix is self-reported and time-specific. It is an example of portfolio design, not a target allocation.

The principle is to combine recurring context-rich work, bounded high-margin diagnostics, and reusable educational assets. Do not add a course because it looks scalable. Add it when the same problem and explanation recur often enough to justify a reusable product.

## A pricing worksheet

Complete this sentence before the next quote:

> For **[buyer]** who must decide **[decision]**, I review **[inputs]**, deliver **[artifact]** within **[time]**, include **[access]**, and exclude **[boundaries]**. The price is **[amount]**.

If any bracket is vague, the offer is not ready to quote. If you need to turn a broad growth problem into a clear scope, review the [current advisory options](/en/services).
